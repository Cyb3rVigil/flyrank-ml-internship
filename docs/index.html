Can Historical Search Signals Prioritize Future Impression-Decline Risk for Unseen Clients?

Abstract. This study asks whether historical search-performance signals can help solve a practical FlyRank content-operations problem: deciding which published client-content pages deserve human investigation first when search visibility quietly deteriorates. Using an anonymized FlyRank Internship Warehouse release, we built five pre-decision numerical features from a historical window and compared a transparent weighted baseline with Logistic Regression and a constrained Random Forest under a client-grouped holdout. On 2,348 eligible client-content rows, the unseen-client test contained 228 rows from four clients with zero training-test client overlap and a 5.7% positive base rate. All three approaches achieved Precision@20 of 0.000 on the honest unseen-client holdout, so the study does not demonstrate useful operational lift for automatic deployment. The resulting recommendation is a transparent, human-reviewed prioritization process while stronger validation data and additional leakage-safe features are collected.

1. Introduction and Problem Statement

FlyRank's content workflow treats content as infrastructure: pages are researched, published, monitored through search data, and optimized over time. A practical problem follows from that workflow: a page can quietly lose search visibility as rankings, clicks, and impressions change, while a team may have thousands of pages competing for limited review attention.

This study turns that product problem into a narrow ranking decision: out of many client-content pages, which twenty should a human analyst investigate first? The output is a review queue, not an automatic publishing action. The research question is whether five historical search-performance features can provide useful prioritization for clients that were completely unseen during model training.

The connection to FlyRank's existing content-operations problem is therefore direct: the experiment tests whether a learned ranking signal can add useful evidence to the page-review workflow while keeping production decisions human-reviewed when the evidence is weak.

2. Data

The analysis uses the pseudonymized FlyRank Internship Warehouse release v20260703, a frozen snapshot exported 3 July 2026, and queries only the March 2026 partition of fact_content_daily_performance. The feature window is 2026-03-01 to 2026-03-21; the non-overlapping outcome window is 2026-03-22 to 2026-03-31. After filtering to at least 14 historical days, at least 7 outcome days, at least 50 historical impressions, and ga4_data_available IS TRUE, 2,348 client-content observations from 14 pseudonymous clients remained. The June 2026 final period and _sample partition were excluded to avoid accidental use of the latest period as an answer key; query-level data and dimension tables were not required for this experiment. The full daily fact contains 78,835,655 rows across the release, but this study models only the filtered March aggregation. Dataset card and data-use terms ↗

The public paper does not expose client names, company names, domains, page URLs, raw search queries, private keywords, credentials, or directly identifying information. Pseudonymous client identifiers are used for grouping and validation, not as predictive features.

3. Methodology

Target definition

A page is labelled positive when its outcome-window average impressions per day falls below 80% of its historical feature-window average impressions per day. This is a proxy for future decline; it does not prove that a page needs a content refresh.

Features

The models use five historical, pre-decision numerical features:

  • Historical impressions per day
  • Historical clicks per day
  • Historical click-through rate
  • Historical average position
  • Historical position standard deviation

Outcome-window variables are excluded from the feature set. Label-derived trend fields and pseudonymous IDs are not used as predictive features.

Models and validation

The comparison includes a transparent weighted baseline, Logistic Regression as the primary learned model, and a constrained Random Forest as a challenger. The final evaluation uses a client-grouped holdout: 2,120 training rows from 10 clients and 228 test rows from four completely unseen clients, with zero client overlap.

4. Results

The honest client-grouped evaluation is the primary result because a random-row split allowed client overlap. That random-row diagnostic produced Precision@20 of 0.700, but 12 clients appeared in both training and test, making it unsuitable as evidence of unseen-client generalization. Under the grouped holdout, Precision@20 fell to 0.000 for all three approaches.

5. Limitations and Honest Framing

Small grouped test set. The client-grouped test contains only 228 rows from four unseen clients and 13 proxy-positive pages, so Precision@20 can be unstable.

Proxy outcome. The label captures a future impressions decline, not the business decision to refresh content. It is observational evidence, not a causal intervention result.

Limited feature set. Only five historical search-performance features are used. The model does not represent every factor that may influence search-performance changes.

No randomized intervention. The study does not establish that any recommended action causes better future performance.

Generalization limits. Four held-out clients are not enough to establish generalization across all industries, content categories, client sizes, or search environments.

6. Ranked Recommendations

  1. Block automatic deployment. Do not let the current model directly change content or publishing decisions.
  2. Use a transparent human-review queue. Prioritize explainable historical signals and show reason codes for each item.
  3. Assign explicit review dispositions. Use REVIEW, INVESTIGATE, TRIAGE, MONITOR, or DEFER so outcomes can be measured consistently.
  4. Treat freshness as a hypothesis. Do not refresh a page only because it is old; require manual verification of the underlying opportunity.
  5. Improve the feature set. Add only pre-decision, public-safe, leakage-checked features such as historical trend, volatility, content age, category, device distribution, or technical availability signals.
  6. Run stronger validation. Add more held-out clients, repeated grouped cross-validation, out-of-time validation, confidence intervals, calibration checks, and multiple label/window definitions.
  7. Measure real interventions. Compare reviewed and untreated pages over consistent 30-day and 60-day follow-up periods.
